Given N=(n1, ..., nk), p, m then exists I subset of {1,...,k} where ∑_{i in I}n_i=m and ∑_{i not in I}n_i=p ?

1) Prove it is in NP

INPUT: (n1, ..., nk), p, m
OUTPUT: True/False

• Certificate: I subset of {1,...,k}
• Verifier:
	sum_not_I=0
	sum_I=0
	for ind in {1...k}:
		if ind in I then
			sum_I += n_ind
		else
			sum_not_I += n_ind
	return (sum_not_I==p) and (sum_I==m)

It works in O(k)

2) Prove it is NP-hard

We can prove that SUBSUM <_p SUBSUM_variation in polynomial time:
In the SUBSUM_variation, we can set ∑_{i in I}n_i=m and ∑_{i not in I}n_i=(∑n_i)-m.
