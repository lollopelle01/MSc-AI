Second-Hand Citation Detector

Flags citations that may have been picked up second-hand rather than engaged with directly — part of CitGraph's transparency layer. Each flag below is a local explanation, not just a score: it shows exactly why a citation was flagged, with the evidence alongside it.

How to read a flagged triad

A candidate is a closed triad u → w → x: paper U cites paper W, W cites paper X, and U also cites X directly. That structural pattern alone is unremarkable — but if U's citing sentence for X reads like an echo of W's citing sentence for X (far more similar than two independent citers of X normally are), and X predates W predates U, it suggests U may have picked up X's framing through W rather than reading X directly. The percentile score is how unusual that similarity is against a background of other citers of the same paper X — 1.00 means U and W's sentences are more alike than any other pair of X's citers.
