Read spearman(risk,interval) as the decision-relevance check: a real
negative correlation (higher risk -> shorter interval) means the decision
layer is actually using the score's own discriminative power, not just
producing cost/DIDI/catch numbers that happen to look reasonable. Compare
this against the fixed policy's undefined/zero correlation (no risk used at
all) and, if re-run at ratio=160 (section 5d), against a correlation that
would collapse toward the fixed policy's as decisions saturate to 3 months.
