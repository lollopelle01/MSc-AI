If the learned model mostly just re-derives RISK_SCORE (that feature dominating
importances) and matches the formula closely, that's a clean negative result:
the extra raw features don't carry decision-relevant information the risk score
hadn't already summarized. If other features carry real weight and the policy
numbers move, that's evidence a learned policy could do genuinely more than the
closed-form formula, at the cost of losing the formula's transparency (section 7's
decision_mechanism.py could no longer explain 'why this interval' as three lines
of arithmetic -- it would need SHAP or similar, same tradeoff stage 2 already has).
