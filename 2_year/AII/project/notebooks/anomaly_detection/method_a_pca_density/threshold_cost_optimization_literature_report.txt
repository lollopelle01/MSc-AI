Cost-based threshold optimization, LITERATURE-ANCHORED cost ratios
(replaces threshold_cost_optimization.py's arbitrary [5, 10, 20] sweep)

Source: 2.8x is the point estimate from a 2026 medRxiv preprint on the
societal cost of Alzheimer's disease by diagnosis timing (~62,000 EUR/yr
early MCI vs. ~175,000 EUR/yr late severe AD). 1.5x/4.0x are the low/high
end of 'Global Societal Burden of Alzheimer's Disease by Severity'
(Neurology and Therapy, 81 studies, 2013-2024, typical ratio 1.4-3.6x).
This is the SAME literature anchor stage 3's cost_model_for() already uses
for MCI_OR_WORSE_COST_MULTIPLIER -- reused here for stage 1's own threshold
instead of an unrelated, arbitrary sweep, so both stages rest on one
consistent, citable real-world cost story instead of two disconnected ones.

Reframing caveat, stated honestly: this literature prices EARLY vs LATE
diagnosis of the same disease, which is exactly what a missed anomaly alarm
causes (later, costlier detection) -- it is not a literal 'one false alarm'
price, so c_alarm is kept at 1 unit (one routine workup) and only the
missed/alarm RATIO is replaced with this real anchor.
